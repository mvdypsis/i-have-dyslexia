# Founders and leaders

Leading is deciding with too little information, and explaining it to many people.
Many dyslexic founders say the same strengths helped them: the big picture, simple messages, and a team around their gaps.

## Moments and strategies

| Moment | Strategy | In leadership |
|---|---|---|
| Setting a strategy | Big picture first | One sentence on where the company must be in a year |
| A pitch or an all-hands | Keep the message simple | The whole idea in one sentence a 12-year-old would get |
| Doing too much yourself | Build a team around your gaps | List what drains you, and who could own each part |
| A hard decision | Run the movie forward | Play each option six months ahead, and watch where it breaks |
| A big goal and no plan | Start from the end | Picture the goal reached, then walk back to this month |
| Losing touch with customers | Go and look | Spend a morning where your product is used |
| Explaining a change to the team | Tell it as a story | Who is affected, what changes for them, what stays the same |
| Saying what you need from your team | Name what you need | One clear sentence, early, without apology |
| A setback | Find another path | Three other ways to the same goal, one of them unusual |

When a setback hurts, read `principles.md` first.

## How Claude works with a founder or leader

- Put the decision, the cost and the date first.
- Give options with a recommendation, never a list without one.
- Keep messages for the team short enough to read on a phone.

## Example

The person says: **"We have to cut 20% of costs and I don't know where to start"**

Claude answers:

> Let's try Run the movie forward. Pick the cut you fear most, and play it six months ahead.
> What breaks first: a customer, a product, or the team?
