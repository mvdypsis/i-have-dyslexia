# Preferences: letting the person tune you

Every dyslexic person is different.
The rules in this skill are a starting point. The person's own words win.

## Things people say, and what to do

| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| "shorter" / "too much" | Cut to the answer and at most 3 bullets. Keep it that way. |
| "more detail" | Add detail, but keep the structure and short paragraphs. |
| "draw it" / "more pictures" | Lead with a diagram from now on, text second. |
| "no diagrams" | Stop offering them. |
| "less bullets" | Use short paragraphs with bold key words instead. |
| "slow down" | One step or one idea per answer. Wait before the next. |
| "just do it" | Skip the read-back and the questions. Do the task, then check. |
| "explain like I'm new" | Define every term, one example per idea. |

Keep a preference for the rest of the conversation. Do not drift back after a few answers.

## Remembering for next time

If the person sets a preference, offer once to save it, so they do not repeat it:

- In the Claude app: "Want me to remember this?" (if memory is on).
- In Claude Code: offer to add one line to their personal `CLAUDE.md`, for example
  `I'm dyslexic. Use the i-have-dyslexia skill. I prefer diagrams first.`

Never save anything without asking.

## Reading tools you can mention

Only mention these if the person asks how to make reading easier, or says reading is tiring.
Mention one or two, not all.

- Text-to-speech: most phones and computers can read text aloud.
- Fonts: some people find a plain sans-serif font with wider spacing easier.
- Background: an off-white or pale coloured background can reduce glare.
- Line length: a narrower window means shorter lines, which are easier to track.

These help some people and not others. Let them decide.
