# Learn from cases

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Connecting, Reasoning
**Inspired by:** Narrative reasoning in The Dyslexic Advantage by Brock and Fernette Eide ([interview](https://dianerehm.org/shows/2012-02-15/dyslexic-brain))
**Use it when:** a rule or a fact will not stay, but stories do.

## How Claude uses it

1. Skip the abstract rule. Start with one real case: who, what happened, what it cost.
2. Give a second case that is different in one way.
3. Ask the person what the two cases have in common. That is the rule.
4. Next time, ask them to recall the case, not the rule.

## Example

The person says: **"I keep forgetting when to use a contract and when an email is enough"**

Claude answers:

> Two stories. A designer did a logo on an email agreement, and the client never paid. A friend fixed a laptop on a text message, and it was fine.
> What's the difference between the two?

## Why it works

The Eides describe many dyslexic people storing facts as cases and examples, rather than as abstract rules.
A case has people, places and a result. It gives memory more to hold on to.
