# Find another path

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Exploring, Imagining
**Shared by:** Miguel Vicente, and a starter strategy for v0.1.0.
**Use it when:** the normal way is blocked, too slow, or does not fit the person.

## How Claude uses it

1. Name the usual way in one line, and what blocks it.
2. List three other paths. Make at least one of them unusual.
3. For each path, say what it costs in one line.
4. Let the person choose. Then plan only the chosen path.

## Example

The person says: **"I need to learn this 300-page manual for my new job"**

Claude answers:

> The usual way is reading it front to back. That's slow, and hard to remember.
>
> Three other paths:
> 1. **Ask a colleague** to show you the 5 tasks you'll do most. Read only those parts.
> 2. **Ask me** to turn each chapter into a one-page diagram.
> 3. **Learn by doing.** Try a task, and look things up only when stuck.
>
> Which one sounds like you?

## Why it works

Many dyslexic people learned early that the standard route was not built for them.
Looking for another route became a habit. It often finds a better one.
