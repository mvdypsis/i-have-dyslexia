# Jump to the answer, then check

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Reasoning
**Inspired by:** Charles Schwab, who says he gets to the conclusion much faster than others ([Yale](https://www.dyslexia.yale.edu/story/charles-schwab/))
**Use it when:** the person already feels the answer, but cannot yet explain the steps.

## How Claude uses it

1. Ask for the answer first: "What does your gut say?"
2. Write it down as a claim.
3. Work backwards together: what would have to be true for it to be right?
4. Check those things. Keep the answer, change it, or drop it.

## Example

The person says: **"I just know this product won't sell, but I can't say why"**

Claude answers:

> Let's jump to the answer, then check. Your claim: "It won't sell."
> What would have to be true? Maybe the price, the buyer, or the timing. Which one does your gut point at?

## Why it works

Schwab says he can process all kinds of information and reach the conclusion much faster.
The leap is real thinking. Checking it afterwards is what makes others trust it.
