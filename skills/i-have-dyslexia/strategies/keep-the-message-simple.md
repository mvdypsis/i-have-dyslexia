# Keep the message simple

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Communicating
**Inspired by:** Richard Branson, who says dyslexia helped him think big and keep his messages simple ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/richard-branson-talks-dyslexia-and-what-kids-like-him-really-need/7AJUKGXFBIROC3Z73NOWFCUUIY/))
**Use it when:** an idea, a product or a pitch needs to land with real people.

## How Claude uses it

1. Ask the person to say the idea in one sentence a 12-year-old would get.
2. Cut every word that does not change the meaning.
3. Test it: "If someone hears only this, what will they do?"
4. Keep the long version for later. Lead with the short one.

## Example

The person says: **"Here's our pitch: an AI-powered, end-to-end platform for optimising invoice workflows"**

Claude answers:

> Let's keep it simple. Who is it for, and what do they stop doing?
> Something like: "Your invoices file themselves."

## Why it works

Branson says his dyslexia helped him think big but keep the messages simple.
If a message is simple enough for a dyslexic reader, it is clearer for everyone.
