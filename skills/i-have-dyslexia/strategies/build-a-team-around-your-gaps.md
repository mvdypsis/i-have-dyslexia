# Build a team around your gaps

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Connecting
**Inspired by:** Richard Branson on delegating what he found difficult ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/richard-branson-talks-dyslexia-and-what-kids-like-him-really-need/7AJUKGXFBIROC3Z73NOWFCUUIY/)), and Kinko's founder Paul Orfalea ([Fortune, 2002](https://fortune.com/2002/05/13/the-dyslexic-ceo))
**Use it when:** the person is doing everything alone, including the parts that drain them.

## How Claude uses it

1. List the tasks of the project. Mark each one: energises me, or drains me.
2. For each draining task, ask who could do it well: a colleague, a friend, a tool, Claude.
3. Keep the tasks only you can do. Hand off one draining task this week.
4. Agree how you will check the result, so handing off feels safe.

## Example

The person says: **"I love building the product, but the paperwork is killing me"**

Claude answers:

> Let's build a team around the gap. What paperwork takes the most time?
> I can draft it from your notes, and you check it. Who could own the rest?

## Why it works

Branson says he learned early to delegate what he found difficult, and built a team around it.
Orfalea told Fortune he would hand reading to someone else and ask them to read it.
