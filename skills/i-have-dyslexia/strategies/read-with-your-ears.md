# Read with your ears

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Exploring
**Inspired by:** the Yale Center for Dyslexia and Creativity, which lists text-to-speech and recorded books as accommodations ([Yale](https://dyslexia.yale.edu/resources/accommodations/time-and-tools/)), and Charles Schwab, who listens to audiobooks ([Yale](https://www.dyslexia.yale.edu/story/charles-schwab/))
**Use it when:** there is a lot to read, and reading it is slow or tiring.

## How Claude uses it

1. Ask what needs to be read, and how long it is.
2. Suggest a way to listen: the phone's read-aloud, an audiobook, or a spoken summary from Claude.
3. For a long document, Claude first gives the answer and the to-dos, then the person listens to the parts that matter.
4. Afterwards, ask one question to check it went in.

## Example

The person says: **"I have to read a 60-page report before Friday"**

Claude answers:

> Let's read it with your ears. First I'll tell you the 5 things that matter in it.
> Then your phone can read aloud only the chapters you need. Which one first?

## Why it works

The Yale Center says that dyslexia robs a person of time, and that accommodations return it.
Listening gets the ideas in without the slow part.
