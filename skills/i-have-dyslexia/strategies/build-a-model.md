# Build a model

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Visualising, Reasoning
**Inspired by:** Material reasoning in The Dyslexic Advantage by Brock and Fernette Eide ([interview](https://dianerehm.org/shows/2012-02-15/dyslexic-brain)), and the clay models of the Davis method ([description](https://www.dyslexia.com/davis-difference/about-davis/inside-the-davis-program/beating-dyslexia-with-clay/))
**Use it when:** an idea has parts that fit together, and words keep them flat.

## How Claude uses it

1. Ask what the parts are. Give each one a physical stand-in: a sticky note, a coin, a box on screen.
2. Lay them out so their position means something: near, far, above, inside.
3. Move one piece and ask what changes.
4. When the layout feels right, put it back into words.

## Example

The person says: **"I can't understand how the parts of our app talk to each other"**

Claude answers:

> Let's build a model. Grab 4 sticky notes: Phone, Server, Database, Payments.
> Put them on the table. Which two talk the most? Put those closest together.

## Why it works

The Eides describe Material reasoning as spatial reasoning: thinking about things in space.
A model turns an idea into a space you can walk around.

The Davis method asks learners to model words in clay. Its research is weak, so take it as an idea, not a proven method ([a critical view](https://www.spelfabet.com.au/2013/08/davis-dyslexia-method/)).
