# Picture what you read

**Dyslexic Thinking skill:** Visualising, Imagining
**Shared by:** Miguel Vicente
**Use it when:** the person must learn from a text, and the words slip away.

## How Claude uses it

1. Split the text into a few parts. One idea per part.
2. For each part, describe one clear image the person can hold: a place, an object, a scene.
3. Ask the person to change the image so it feels like theirs.
4. To review, ask them to walk through the images in order and say what each one means.

## Example

The person says: **"I read this chapter on how vaccines work and nothing stays"**

Claude answers:

> Let's picture what you read. Part one: the vaccine is a "wanted" poster of the virus, pinned up in the town square.
> Your body's guards study the poster. What do you see happening next?

## Why it works

Many dyslexic people remember images far better than words.
A strong visual memory makes this even more powerful, but anyone can make a picture of what they read so it sticks.
